1. Synthetic data - it is data that are generated by generative AI algorithms, based on real-life mathematical models or other rules and may represent real-world data. May be used for educational purposes, researches and training of new AI models
2. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are a deep learning architecture that involves two competing AI models tasked with generating highly realistic data (such as text, video, audio, or images) based on an initial training dataset. These models subsequently evaluate each other’s output, attempting to determine whether the data is new—generated by the opposing network—or part of the original dataset.
3. Foundation model - a large AI model pre-trained on broad, general-purpose data that can later be adapted (with little extra training) to many different downstream tasks. In my report, SensorFM and Harrison.Rad 1.5 are foundation models: one model serves many healthcare tasks instead of one model per task.
4. Fine-tuning - the process of taking an already pre-trained model and continuing its training on a smaller, task-specific dataset so it behaves better for that exact use case (e.g., fine-tuning a general language model on clinical trial data).
5. EHR (Electronic Health Record) - the digital version of a patient’s paper chart: diagnoses, treatments, lab results, notes. EHRs are largely unstructured text, which is why generative AI is useful for extracting insights from them.
6. PII (Personal Identifiable Information) - any data that can directly or indirectly identify a person (name, ID, photos, medical data). Because training datasets contain PII, they must be protected under regulations like HIPAA or GDPR.
7. Model bias - a systematic error that appears when a model learns from data that already contains historical inequalities (e.g., uneven medical records across population groups), so the model reproduces or amplifies those inequalities in its outputs.
8. Explainability - the degree to which a human can understand why an AI system produced a particular output. In high-stakes fields (healthcare, finance, security) a "black box" answer is not acceptable, so explainability is a key requirement for adoption.
9. Transformer - the neural network architecture behind modern generative AI. It processes whole sequences (words, pixels) using a mechanism called self-attention, which lets the model weigh how every element relates to every other element. GPT stands for Generative Pre-trained Transformer.
10. GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) - a family of large generative models by OpenAI built on the transformer architecture, trained on huge text corpora to understand and generate human-like language.
11. NLP (Natural Language Processing) - the field of AI focused on understanding and generating human language. Generative AI brought a shift in NLP from classifying/extracting text to producing fluent, contextually relevant language.
12. Data augmentation - artificially expanding a training dataset by creating modified or synthetic copies of existing samples (e.g., generating synthetic X-rays) so models train on more diverse data without collecting new real data.
13. De-identification - removing or replacing personal identifiers from data so it can no longer be linked to a real person, allowing datasets to be shared for research without violating privacy laws.
14. Model drift - the gradual degradation of a deployed model’s performance because real-world data changes over time (new threats, new user behavior), so the model must be periodically retrained, validated and tuned.
15. Human-in-the-loop (HITL) - a workflow design where a human reviews, validates and approves AI outputs before they take effect (e.g., developers validating AI-generated code, doctors reviewing AI-drafted reports). It is the main safeguard against AI errors.
16. Deep learning - a family of machine learning methods based on multi-layer neural networks that learn complex, non-linear patterns directly from large amounts of data; the foundation of GANs and transformers.
17. Prompt - a natural-language instruction or question given to a generative model to get a result (e.g., "convert this COBOL module to Java"). Prompt quality directly determines output quality.
18. Chatbot - a software application that simulates conversation with users. Generative AI made chatbots capable of free-form, context-aware dialogue instead of scripted menu trees.
19. Virtual assistant - an AI-powered application (chat or voice based) that provides personalized services to end users: managing bank accounts (Erica), shopping recommendations (Alexa), or tailored tutoring (Duolingo’s AI Tutor).
20. Anomaly detection - identifying data points or behaviors that deviate from the learned "normal" pattern (e.g., unusual spikes in network traffic). Generative models help by learning what normal looks like and flagging what does not fit.
